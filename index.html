In the educational environmental comic titled "TRASH", a simple story unfolds about a boy named Geo resting at a park with his mother. As his mother picks up food packaging, Geo asks: "But Mom, if it's just one small wrapper, what's wrong with throwing it here? Everyone else does it too!"

The child's innocent question encapsulates the core root of today's environmental crisis in Indonesia and around the world. A seemingly minor choice—"just one plastic wrapper"—causes immense ecological and social harm.

1. Reflections from the 'TRASH' Comic: The Math of 'Just One Piece' & Social Proof

💬 Why Geo's Mindset in the Comic is Extremely Dangerous

In the comic, the mother wisely points out: when we litter under the excuse of following others, it triggers a chain reaction of social proof. One person sees the first piece of trash, considers it acceptable to drop a second, then a third, and so on.

The ultimate outcome? A once green and vibrant park quickly transforms into a filthy, smelly dumping site where children can no longer play safely.

Now, let's scale the logic of this comic up to national and global levels. Indonesia alone is home to over 275 million people. If every single person thinks "Oh, it's just 1 plastic wrapper", that yields 275 million plastic wrappers littering streets, waterways, and urban parks every single day.

Globally, with a population exceeding 8 billion people, the mindset of "just 1 trash item" generates 8 billion loose plastic items polluting Planet Earth every day!

2. The Reality of the Waste Crisis in Indonesia

Official data from Indonesia's Ministry of Environment and Forestry (KLHK) indicates that Indonesia produces approximately 68.5 million tons of waste annually, with 18% or about 12.3 million tons consisting of single-use plastic waste.

Much of this waste is improperly managed in landfills and ends up polluting terrestrial and marine ecosystems:

  • Severe Urban Flooding: Drainage systems and rivers are choked by thousands of plastic bags and bottles, causing annual floods in Jakarta, Surabaya, Bandung, and other major hubs.
  • Microplastics in the Food Chain: Marine plastic waste degrades into microplastics, eaten by fish, and ultimately ingested by humans through seafood consumption.
  • Wildlife & Sea Turtle Mortality: Hundreds of marine species become entangled or die after swallowing plastic debris mistaken for food.

3. Hazards of Open Waste Burning & Ozone Layer Depletion

Overwhelmed by mounting piles of trash caused by widespread littering, communities often resort to a quick fix: burning plastic waste in the open.

⚠️ Why Burning Plastic Waste is an Atmospheric Disaster

Plastics are synthesized from petroleum compounds (petrochemicals). When burned openly without specialized industrial incinerators, plastic emits deadly toxins including Dioxins, Furans, Carbon Monoxide (CO), and Greenhouse Gases (CO2 & Methane).

The smoke from open plastic burning not only triggers acute respiratory infections (ARI) and cancer, but also contributes massive greenhouse gas emissions into the atmosphere. These emissions accelerate global warming, deplete Earth's protective ozone layer, and exacerbate extreme climate events.

4. Conclusion & Actionable Steps for Students

Geo's story in the 'TRASH' comic serves as a mirror: environmental change always begins with individual choices, including those we make as students. We cannot keep pointing fingers at others or blaming authorities while continuing to litter at school or in our local neighborhoods.

🌏 DON'T LET OUR EARTH REACH ITS "FINAL SEASON"!

"Remember: If every 1 person litters 1 plastic wrapper, there will be 8 Billion plastic wastes (matching the human population on Earth) polluting this planet. And if this trash is burned carelessly, ozone depletion and global warming will push Earth into its FINAL SEASON (Climate Doom)."

Let us as the younger generation break this cycle of bad habits right now! Hold onto your trash in your pocket or bag until you find a proper trash bin. Protecting the Earth means protecting our own future.
